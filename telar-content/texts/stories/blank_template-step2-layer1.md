---
title: "Government Decrees Require the General Means to Execute Them"
---

Rodriguez makes an important distinction when he says "Instruir no es EDUCAR." For him to simply teach someone a skill without the proper education to follow is not a good precedent to set. It gives a false sense of empowerment and doesn't fully prepare a person to participate in society. He explains that a person can know military strategy without being a soldier, the same way a person can be given instruction without being properly educated as well. That education has a much higher social purpose. The distinction is important to Rodriguez's vision of a new government because changing the political system alone would not be enough. The people within that system would also need to be educated in a way that prepares them to participate and maintain the new society. Without that education the new government would exist politically, but the society needed to make it function would not.
